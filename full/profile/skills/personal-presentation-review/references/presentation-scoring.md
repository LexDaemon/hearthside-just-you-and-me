# Presentation scoring

Score the current visible presentation in its stated context, not the person. Use whole numbers and tie every axis to an observation.

## Weights

| Axis | Weight | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Fit and silhouette | 22 | Garment fit, shape, drape, length, layering, structure |
| Proportion and visual balance | 18 | Visible lines, volume, focal hierarchy, posture |
| Colour and contrast | 15 | Outfit harmony and apparent interaction with visible hair/skin under current light |
| Coherence and context | 15 | Formality, purpose, footwear/accessories, visual consistency |
| Appearance and grooming | 15 | Visible hair, beard, facial framing, maintenance, finish |
| Finish and condition | 15 | Wrinkles, lint, fabric/shoe condition, detail coherence |

For scores 9–10, the visible axis is strongly intentional and supports the context. Scores 7–8 are clearly good with limited refinements. Scores 5–6 are mixed with a material repair available. Scores 3–4 have high-impact visible conflict. Scores 1–2 have dominant mismatch/condition/fit failure. Never manufacture severity.

## Coverage and calculation

- Mark an axis `N/A` only when it is entirely unobservable.
- Coverage is the sum of visible weights: **high** at 80–100, **medium** at 55–79, **low** below 55.
- At low coverage, provide a partial visible review and no total.
- Otherwise calculate `round(sum(weight × axis score) / sum(visible weights))` with an available calculation tool. Never calculate it mentally.
- State the reason for coverage: missing footwear, cropped lower body, unknown context, unreliable light, and similar material limits.

## Evidence rule

A valid negative point has three parts:

```text
visible observation → visual effect → smallest practical repair
```

Describe photo-specific facts rather than asserting a permanent skin undertone, face shape, body type, or personal trait.
