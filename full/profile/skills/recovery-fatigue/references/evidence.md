# Evidence standard for recovery and fatigue

Recovery is a context-dependent decision problem. Do not reduce it to a single wearable score, soreness rating, supplement, or ritual. Use reported function, performance trend, technique, symptoms, sleep/stress context, and abrupt load or diet changes together.

## Decision standard

- A one-off bad session is weak evidence for a major intervention.
- Repeated decline plus worsening effort, technique, sleep, or symptoms deserves a conservative load decision and reassessment.
- Symptom relief is not the same as restored capacity.
- Keep triage non-diagnostic. Persistent, severe, systemic, neurological, cardiopulmonary, or function-limiting symptoms need appropriate professional assessment.

## Foundational sources to verify when relevant

- Kellmann M, et al. *Recovery and Performance in Sport: Consensus Statement* (2018). PMID: 29345524. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29345524/
- Meeusen R, et al. *Prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of the overtraining syndrome: joint consensus statement* (2013). PMID: 23247672. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23247672/
- Wiewelhove T, et al. *A Meta-Analysis of the Effects of Foam Rolling on Performance and Recovery* (2019). PMID: 31024339. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31024339/

Use current evidence for a particular recovery device, sleep claim, or medical-adjacent assertion rather than extrapolating broadly from these sources.
